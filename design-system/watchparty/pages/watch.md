# Watch page — theater and chat

Implemented 2026-11-13; theater, polls, and paired palettes updated 2026-11-17. This page overrides the older screen outlines in MASTER.md. The reference-driven theater plan is in [theater.md](theater.md).

## Product hierarchy

1. Room identity and connection: the room name is the page h1, immediately to the right of the WatchParty logo in the topmost bar; connection state follows on its right. Hosts edit inline: the name becomes a focused, selected textbox and its adjacent pencil becomes a save checkmark. Enter/check saves, Escape cancels, and acknowledgment returns focus to the pencil. Names are validated to 80 characters; rejected drafts and linked errors remain in place. Pending saves prevent duplicates/cancellation; losing host access ends editing. Updates reach everyone in the room and the directory; viewers cannot rename rooms. Mobile editing temporarily frees space from theme/browse controls in the same bar. No separate room masthead or decorative labels.
2. Room actions: Invite friends and Room permissions (hosts) use named icon buttons to the right of the display name and its edit pencil. The Leave room button is removed. No general Room options menu. Invitations retain their named dialog, labeled selectable link, clipboard fallback, and focus return. These actions remain available in fullscreen.

Logo and Browse rooms navigation during active/processing video share the same leave confirmation. The video provider sees Stop sharing and leave? with the consequence that playback stops for everyone; other viewers see Leave watch party?. Stay in room receives initial focus. Cancelling/Escape preserves the connection and restores focus to whichever link opened the dialog. Confirmed room departure uses the room leave endpoint before disconnecting and returning to the hub; pending actions prevent duplicate submits and errors remain retryable. Idle-room navigation and modified clicks that open another tab retain normal link behavior. The logo has an accessible name even when mobile hides the brand text.

While this connected browser supplies the video, refresh, tab/window close, and other document exits request the browser's native `beforeunload` confirmation. Cancelling does no cleanup or network mutation; `pagehide` retains responsibility for actual departure. The guard follows video ownership rather than host role, including preparation and host transfer, and is removed when sharing ends, the connection is lost, or the header unmounts. A successful in-app confirmed leave bypasses the native prompt to avoid asking twice; failed leave requests retain protection. Per [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/beforeunload_event), browsers supply generic wording, require prior user interaction, and cannot reliably warn on all mobile termination paths.
3. Shared screen: the video leads the composition. Its compact toolbar contains the filename/source name, a plain-language playback summary, and adjacent Choose video / Replace video and Share screen actions. Screen capture opens the native screen/window/tab picker and becomes Stop sharing while active. Picker cancellation preserves the source. The capture provider keeps Stop sharing after a host transfer. Movi renders both live shares and files with consistent local volume/pause and theater fullscreen controls. Live shares disable seeking, speed, file quality, and captions; returning to a file restores its permitted controls. Keep provider reminders visible and extended instructions in Playback & sharing details. See [screen sharing](../../../docs/screen-sharing.md) for audio/browser and relay requirements.
4. Chatroom identity: the current display name replaces the former Good company / The lounge heading. The 44px pencil sits immediately after the name. Editing replaces the name with an inline textbox and the pencil with a checkmark; Enter/check saves, Escape cancels. Save waits for server acknowledgment, prevents duplicate sends, and preserves rejected drafts with linked inline errors. Pending saves cannot be cancelled; disconnection/restriction feedback remains visible. Success/cancellation returns focus to the pencil. Invite and permissions remain aligned at the right of the heading.
5. Chatroom navigation: Chat, People, and Up next use the same tab structure at every width. People carries the participant count in a small circular badge; Chat carries an identical unread-message badge only when there are unread messages. Counts above 99 display as 99+ while accessible names retain the full count. Read messages clear through the existing attention tracking. Selected tabs and drafts survive width changes. Hidden panels stay mounted; the player and conversation never remount because a tab changed.

## Layout

- Desktop/tablet at 768px+: edge-to-edge viewport with one compact brand/room bar, flexible black video stage, and a resizable chat column defaulting to 380px. The edge toggle is also the drag handle, with keyboard arrows, Home/End, and cancellation. Width and open/closed state persist locally; narrower viewports clamp temporarily. Filename and Choose/Replace video sit above the stage. Use flat adjoining surfaces rather than rounded cards or large gutters.
- Below 768px: video above chat; chat fills the remaining viewport and keeps its composer visible. Short landscape viewports at least 600px wide retain side-by-side video/chat with a narrower dock. Room-action labels shorten visibly while their accessible names retain their full context.
- A persistent 44px edge chevron hides/reopens the entire chat panel and exposes its expanded state and unread count. Escape from chat closes the dock and returns focus to that button; dialogs and popovers keep their own Escape handling. Hidden chat remains mounted, preserving drafts, scroll, votes, and the selected tab. Messages remain unread while the dock or Chat tab is hidden.
- The edge button retains a 44px hit area while its visual surface tucks to a 14px sliver. Hover, keyboard focus, and active dragging reveal it; touch/coarse pointers keep it visible. A 6px horizontal movement threshold distinguishes dragging from clicking, and drag release never toggles chat. Dragging left from closed reopens it. Escape while editing the display name cancels that edit before any chat-close handling.
- Each tab label and its count form one unbroken inline group. Small docks hide redundant tab icons; complete tabs may reflow when text grows, while counts stay beside their labels.
- The viewport contains one relevant content scroller: messages for Chat, roster for People, movie suggestions/results for Up next. The composer remains in the chat panel's flex layout. Reduced motion removes the brief panel-entry transform.
- The chatroom heading uses 8px top / 4px bottom padding, 12px horizontal insets, and 44px controls. Tabs have a 44px minimum height and 4px vertical padding. This removes 32px of vertical chrome while preserving readable labels and touch targets.
- Fullscreen targets the whole theater, keeps the same video and social panel mounted, and hides page/room/media chrome. The edge toggle, Chat/People/Up next tabs, and composer remain available; collapsed chat gives the video the whole screen. Overlay portals move inside the fullscreen root and return on exit. The adapter retains its standalone-shell fallback and mobile pseudo-fullscreen support.

## Content and interaction

- Chat uses the visible Reply / react control, quoted replies, reaction counts, draft preservation, and latest-message navigation. Read-only/disconnected states explain why sending is unavailable.
- The composer uses one compact row with 8px vertical / 12px horizontal padding. Message remains a screen-reader label; omit the redundant visible label. Keep 16px input text, 44px control targets, and visible reply/error/restriction feedback. Empty-video metadata omits repeated start instructions; Choose video remains the action.
- An open movie poll appears in Chat as a compact invitation to vote. Poll creation, search, suggestions, results, and finishing live under Up next. Start poll appears in the empty/results view; starting keeps that tab selected and focuses Movie title.
- Active polls show a heading, contextual vote total, and compact choice cards before the suggestion editor. Cards include poster or SVG fallback, owner, vote count/percentage, meter, selected-vote state, and a quiet named removal control. Movie details reveals descriptions and source links. Suggest a movie reveals search when requested; an empty poll opens the editor immediately. Up next has one scroller.
- Finish poll opens a native confirmation focused on Keep voting. Results show winner/tie cards, All results, and Start another poll. Starting, closing search, cancelling/confirming finishing, and results transitions preserve logical focus. Permission restrictions retain readable viewing states.
- People uses readable, full-width participant rows, initials, role, and self indicators. Compact details open the individual-permission dialog or moderation confirmation established in stage 2.
- Omit redundant Now playing, In this room, and decorative movie-vote eyebrow labels. The filename, selected People tab, and meaningful empty/result headings provide the context. Keep People as a screen-reader heading for the standalone roster.
- Use the existing semantic palette and system font. Uniform SVG stroke style, 44px controls, readable 14px supporting text and 16px inputs/messages. Keep playback errors explicit in the video placeholder instead of leaving a stale Opening video message.
- One h1, named tab panels, arrow/Home/End tab navigation, skip link, visible focus, and focus-returning native dialogs.
- Badge numbers are decorative duplicates of the contextual tab names. A single atomic, polite status announces unread totals; bare counts are not competing live regions. Tabs can wrap their contents when text is enlarged.

- A blocked browser autoplay attempt exposes Join playback within the player; the action resumes locally against the shared timestamp without broadcasting a room command.
